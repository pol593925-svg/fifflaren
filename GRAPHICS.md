# Counter-Strike graphics — 3.25.0

Built on client 3.24.3 (`0dd24b9`), preserving its map layouts and multiplayer protocol.
The old generated world and cube characters are replaced by Blender exports.
This is an original CS:GO-inspired art pass, not the Source engine or a 1:1 replica.

- Dust2 and Inferno: authored facade details, skyline, site markings and PBR surfaces.
- Separate articulated T/CT operators, gloves, sleeves and 19 weapon meshes.
- glTF metallic/roughness and normal maps, sunlight, stabilized shadows and sky reflections.
- Local assets; no texture/CDN requests while playing.
- Escape menu: high, balanced and performance graphics settings.
- Map switches free owned GPU resources and cancel stale asynchronous loads.
- Existing radar, smoke, hit feedback, bots, Gun Game, buying and weapon mechanics remain.
- Stair elevation now rises toward its adjacent tower/bridge, matching the new visible slope.

## Rebuild the artwork

Requires Blender 4.5 LTS. Run from the repository folder. Use an absolute source path.

```powershell
./tools/blender/download_textures.ps1
blender -b --python tools/blender/build_maps.py -- --sources C:/path/to/blender-sources
blender -b --python tools/blender/build_characters.py -- --output models/csgo --source C:/path/to/blender-sources --preview
blender -b --python tools/blender/build_weapons.py -- --sources C:/path/to/blender-sources
node tools/validate-graphics.cjs
```

The generators export packaged GLBs plus manifests. `.blend` source files contain
embedded textures; generated copies are supplied separately from the installer.
See [ART_CREDITS.md](ART_CREDITS.md) for texture provenance.

```powershell
npm ci

```

Browser smoke checks use a mocked Socket.IO connection and never contact the live
server or database. They cover both maps, resource cleanup across rapid switches,
shop controls, quality selection and rendered geometry heights.
The browser check uses installed Chrome; set `CHROME_PATH` for a different Chromium
executable and `GRAPHICS_OUTPUT` to select a screenshot/report directory.

needs a live multi-client playtest; software-rendered QA does not establish gaming FPS.

## 3.44.2 architecture, weapon finishes and companions

Map facade details stay below the existing 4 m roof/collision plane. The former
per-face random upper stories were incomplete; both exports now use a continuous
cornice, shutters, glazing and paneled doors. Thin facade layers omit buried faces:
Dust2 is 345,031 triangles / 49,984,548 bytes, Inferno 275,265 / 42,044,552.
The 75 m flat border outside the arena prevents hills from cutting through skyline
houses and the landmark tower. Walkable two-storey buildings retain their stairs.

Blender weapon materials prefixed `Weapon finish /` are paintable. All other
materials (optical glass, barrel steel, hardware, rubber and gloves) stay original;
`cs16-weapon-finish.js` preserves material arrays and normal/roughness maps.
Weapon and gameplay statistics are unchanged.

`cs16-footsteps.js` caches four heel/toe variations per terrain at the audio device's
sample rate, with at most 16 active voices. The landscape mask selects grass,
earth or paving; vents use metal. Steps follow actual travel distance; Shift
remains silent. Nearby remote steps pan relative to the listener's facing.

Companions use FPS forward `(-sin(yaw), -cos(yaw))`, 2.35 m ahead and .95 m left.
The existing wall/floor contracts shorten the cosmetic path at walls or ledges;
no companion collision or new networking changes are introduced. Visual gait
stops when stationary; sharp turns and respawns place the pet back in front.

Regression checks: `node tools/presentation.test.cjs`; actual GLB/material screenshots
are generated by `PRESENTATION_ONLY=1 node tools/graphics-smoke.cjs`. The full browser
smoke and native Electron load/recovery checks also apply.

## Unpublished hand, arsenal and companion detail pass

The complete nineteen-weapon anime set has baked vertex cavity shading. Smooth
magazine shells retain the magazine animation pivot; nails and joint creases use
the existing four hand grips. First-person hands remain below 2 MiB, and other
players continue to use the existing two-batch weapon LODs.

Companion source files stay below 512 KiB each. Their nested head/ear/eye pivots
are converted to bones and a single shared skinned mesh at load time. Each clone
has independent bones; removal frees its bone texture, preserving shared assets.
Loading prepares all five pet geometries/shaders. Idle breathing, blinking,
ear movement, a glance toward the owner and eased gait do not affect gameplay.

Checks: `validate-graphics.cjs`, `weapon-grip-qa.cjs`, `ANIME_MATCH_ONLY=1`,
`PET_DETAIL_ONLY=1` and `ROUND_FX_ONLY=1` in the real local client. Both maps,

fixture with ten players/pets and spell bursts measured 179–180 FPS on GTX 1080.

it measured 46–49 FPS, p95 33–37 ms and worst frame 64 ms. This emulates a slower
CPU, not a low-end GPU or a live network match; it is not a no-stutter guarantee.

## Confirmed target impacts — 8 November 2026

The combat HUD now distinguishes ivory body hits, cyan armor impacts, gold
headshots and coral eliminations. A short crosshair marker and damage total
accompany painted contact stars, expanding rings and brush-stroke sparks on
the target. Armor-break and fatal-headshot labels use confirmed server data.
Local ray prediction alone never displays damage. Optional shot IDs correlate
accepted server hits with their exact predicted contact, including after a
rejected shot; older server events retain a compatible fallback.

The previous per-hit canvas/texture damage sprites are removed. The new effect
uses three reusable instanced meshes, with twelve contact slots or six on low
quality, and no per-hit GPU resource allocation. Low quality uses three sparks
per contact instead of six. Shaders are prepared behind the loading screen.
Off-screen and distant sparks are rejected before wall/smoke queries, while
confirmed personal damage stays on the HUD. Walls/depth testing and smoke checks
suppress hidden target effects. Round,
map and graphics-context changes clear or dispose the same buffers and HUD.
Weapon balance, map geometry, hand models and pets are unchanged in this pass.

Six focused unit tests and twenty-four server combat/loading/magic/round tests
passed. The real-client browser fixture verifies an emitted shot without an
early hit marker, all four confirmed states, expiry, other players' hits,
zero-damage events, fixed resources after 1,500 hits and map cleanup. Screenshots
in `graphics-qa/anime-hit-*.png` are actual local game captures with scripted
network acknowledgements, not a live employee match. Native Electron startup,
180-explosion resource stability, WebGL recovery and ADS alignment also passed.
The planned 6.0.0 notes include this feature; no release was published.

The stress fixture can now compare the same map with and without repeated
confirmed hits and records hit-handler/update costs separately. At 4x CPU
throttling, low quality, 1280x720 and GTX 1080, the comparison measured 24–39 FPS
across baseline/effect passes; the effect frame update was 0.7–1.0 ms at p95,
and a batch of nine confirmations was 3.4–4.0 ms at p95. Overall frame times
varied considerably even without impacts, so these results do not establish
stable low-end performance or attribute the scene's remaining stalls solely
to the new effects. See `graphics-qa/hit-feedback-performance.json`.
